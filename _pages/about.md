I'm an engineer with 20+ years of 
experience developing advanced numerical 
methods for simulating fluid flows in 
which heat, chemical species, and 
electric fields interact with 
complex surfaces, such as 
porous media, membranes, and particle 
suspensions. My work is motivated 
by applications to desalination, 
carbon sequestration, laminar flow 
control, microrobots, and 
targeted drug delivery. I also
leverage a strong foundation in applied 
mathematics to develop reduced-order 
models for complex systems that 
can't be efficiently modeled with 
direct simulation. These reduced 
models often run 100 to 1000 
times faster than CFD.


I earned my Ph.D. in Mechanical 
Engineering from McGill University, 
followed by postdoctoral research 
at the University of Aix-Marseille 
and the University of Maryland. 
From there, I spent over a decade 
on the faculty at the Colorado School 
of Mines, most recently as Associate 
Professor of Mechanical Engineering. 
I have worked in multi-institution 
research collaborations spanning universities, 
national labs, and industry. My research 
has been funded by the U.S. National 
Science Foundation, Department of Energy, 
and Bureau of Reclamation, including 
an NSF CAREER Award.

My numerical work focuses on building 
algorithms and codes (C++, Fortran, MPI) 
for scientific computing, using finite-volume, 
finite-difference, spectral, and immersed 
boundary methods. My analytical work 
focuses on perturbation methods,
linear stability analysis, and 
volume-averaging. I also work 
extensively in Python and MATLAB, and 
have growing experience with OpenFOAM 
and Fluent. I place strong emphasis on 
code verification and validation, 
including spatio-temporal accuracy 
assessment, mesh-independence studies, 
and comparison with experiments.

Alongside my research, I've developed
and delivered graduate courses in fluid mechanics, 
engineering mathematics, and computational 
fluid dynamics. Out of that teaching 
grew a set of free, open-access textbooks 
that I'm gradually publishing here. 
These textbooks were developed to
reduce my students' financial burden 
and address a growing gap between 
the math engineers learn in their 
undergrad, and the math required in
graduate-level coursework and 
research.

I'm now looking to bring my 
expertise in numerical methods, 
software development, and applied 
math to industry R&D.
